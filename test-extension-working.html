Test Text 1: Simple Process

The water cycle is a continuous process that involves evaporation, condensation, and precipitation. First, water from oceans and lakes evaporates due to heat from the sun. Next, the water vapor rises into the atmosphere where it cools and condenses into clouds. Finally, when the clouds become heavy enough, precipitation occurs in the form of rain, snow, or hail, returning water to the Earth's surface.

Test Text 2: Historical Timeline

The Renaissance period began in Italy during the 14th century and spread throughout Europe. In 1401, Filippo Brunelleschi won the competition for the Florence Baptistery doors. By 1503, Leonardo da Vinci had completed the Mona Lisa. In 1512, Michelangelo finished painting the Sistine Chapel ceiling. The Renaissance continued until the 17th century, influencing art, science, and philosophy across Europe.

Test Text 3: Cause and Effect

Climate change is caused by increased greenhouse gas emissions from human activities. These emissions trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, leading to global temperature rise. As temperatures increase, polar ice caps melt, causing sea levels to rise. Rising sea levels result in coastal flooding and displacement of communities. Additionally, climate change affects weather patterns, leading to more frequent and severe storms, droughts, and heatwaves.

Test Text 4: Comparison

Renewable energy sources like solar and wind power offer several advantages over fossil fuels. Solar energy is clean and abundant, but requires large land areas and depends on weather conditions. Wind energy is cost-effective and produces no emissions, yet can be noisy and requires specific locations. Fossil fuels are reliable and energy-dense, but produce harmful emissions and are finite resources. Nuclear power provides consistent energy output but poses safety and waste disposal challenges.
